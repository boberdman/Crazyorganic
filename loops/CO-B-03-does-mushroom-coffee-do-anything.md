## A · TOPIC BRIEF (Research → Content)

**Pillar:** 4 — What's real vs. what's sold (grounding)
**Working title:** Does Mushroom Coffee Do Anything?
**Big question / hook:** Mushroom coffee is the functional-mushroom product most people actually buy. Does it do anything? The honest answer isn't chemistry — it's arithmetic: how much mushroom is in the cup versus how much the studies used. Once you do the napkin math, the category snaps into focus, and the one reliable effect turns out to be the caffeine you're *not* drinking.
**Why now (trend note):** Mushroom coffee is a durable, still-growing wellness trend (persistent TikTok/retail presence through 2025–26). It's the natural third beat of Series B: CO-B-01 walked the aisle, CO-B-02 deep-dived lion's mane the *species*; this deep-dives the *delivery format* everyone buys — and its spine (dose math + the caffeine confound) is new to the series, not a repeat of the lion's-mane evidence review.
**Key points:**
1. What's sold: coffee (instant/ground arabica) + powdered mushroom extracts — headline four are lion's mane (focus), cordyceps (energy), chaga (immunity), reishi (calm), often plus turkey tail/shiitake/king trumpet and non-fungal adaptogens (rhodiola, ashwagandha). Under DSHEA: "supports focus/immune health" allowed, disease claims not; FDA disclaimer required. [established re: product + regulation]
2. **The dose spine (the new, load-bearing beat):** trials that showed *any* effect used gram-scale daily doses of a *single* mushroom — lion's mane ~1.8 g (acute) to ~3 g/day (Mori 2009); cordyceps CS-4 ~1–3 g/day. A serving of mushroom coffee typically delivers a fraction: one disclosed blend = 250 mg lion's mane (~1/12 of the 3 g trial dose); another = a 2 g six-mushroom blend with no per-species breakdown; many disclose nothing per species. "Contains lion's mane" ≠ "an effective dose of lion's mane." [established — trial doses + disclosed label amounts]
3. **The caffeine confound (the honest "it does do something"):** mushroom coffee usually has less caffeine (~50–60 mg vs ~95 mg standard cup). Less caffeine is a real felt effect (fewer jitters, gentler curve) — the most likely reason people notice a difference, and it isn't the mushrooms. Feeling different ≠ the advertised ingredient working. [established]
4. **What's in the jar (carried from CO-B-02):** actives = beta-glucans (+ lion's mane hericenones/erinacines), concentrated in fruiting-body extract; mycelium-on-grain dilutes them with starch, so a gram of powder can carry far fewer actives — and extract type is often undisclosed, lowering the real dose beneath the label number. [established that fruiting body > mycelium-on-grain; contested/variable per product]
5. **Does any of it work, at the right size:** lion's mane = promising/unproven at gram doses (see CO-B-02) [preliminary]; cordyceps = older-adult threshold gains at 1–3 g but *no* benefit in trained cyclists at ~3.15 g — results disagree across populations [contested]; chaga (immune/antioxidant) mostly cell/animal, thin human data, reishi (calm) small human + tradition [preliminary]; blends-as-sold essentially untested as blends, no long-term data [preliminary].
6. **Safety edges:** generally well tolerated at coffee amounts, but chaga is oxalate-rich with documented case reports of oxalate nephropathy/ESRD tied to heavy long-term use; possible drug interactions; allergy risk. "Natural ≠ inert." Held conservatively; no dosing-as-instruction. [established that case reports exist + chaga oxalate-rich; rarity/dose-dependence hedged]
**Epistemic status:** an **Established-grounded, Preliminary/Contested-efficacy** piece — good balance for Pillar 4. The load-bearing spine (dose math, regulation, caffeine, disclosed labels) is Established; efficacy of the mushrooms is Preliminary; cordyceps cross-population and extract-vs-mycelium are Contested. **No Established efficacy claim is made.**
**Sources to use:** Medical News Today (mushroom-coffee evidence overview); FDA Structure/Function (DSHEA); Biology Insights (caffeine ~50–60 mg; lion's-mane 1.8–3.2 g; extraction/beta-glucan); Mushroom Atlas (per-serving label amounts: Four Sigmatic Focus 250 mg LM + 250 mg chaga fruiting body; Ryze 2 g/6-species undisclosed); ADDF Cognitive Vitality "Lion's Mane" (carried from CO-B-02); NutriDetector Cordyceps CS-4 (trial doses + cyclist-null); chaga oxalate-nephropathy case reports (PubMed 35451393; JKMS 2020).
**Claims-risk flag:** **HEALTH-CLAIM → ESCALATE (permanent §5 gate).** Series B / Theme D is permanently Tier 1 (Config §4 #3, §5; Ledger hard-line register). No §4 #1/#2 (no psychedelics — chaga/cordyceps/lion's mane/reishi are non-psychoactive). Handling notes carried to Content: (a) never assert efficacy — frame every benefit as preliminary/contested and under-dosed; (b) lead with dose math, not chemistry; (c) name the caffeine confound explicitly; (d) no dosing framed as instruction, no "drink this" nudge; (e) keep the "not medical advice / see a clinician" beat; (f) carry the chaga/kidney + allergy edges without alarmism.

**X posts:**

1. *(hook / the math)* Mushroom coffee, honestly: it's not a chemistry question, it's arithmetic. The studies that showed anything used **grams** of a single mushroom a day. Your cup holds a few hundred **milligrams** — often split across six mushrooms, often undisclosed. "Contains lion's mane" ≠ "a dose of lion's mane that does anything." 🧵 — *Established*

2. *(the confound)* But people *do* feel something — calmer, less wired. Real effect, wrong cause: mushroom coffee usually has ~half the caffeine of a normal cup. Less caffeine feels like something. You just credit the exotic word on the bag instead of the boring one that quietly went down. Feeling different isn't proof the mushroom worked. — *Established*

3. *(the honest turn)* Do the mushrooms do anything? Maybe — at doses your coffee doesn't reach. Lion's mane: promising, unproven. Cordyceps: helped older adults, did nothing for trained cyclists. Blends: never tested as blends. Pleasant, probably harmless, under-dosed for the claim. Enjoy the mug; keep the awe for the mushrooms. Not medical advice. — *Preliminary / Contested*
